P31 normal 1.	Introduction
P32 Heading 2 1.1	Purpose
P33 normal This document specifies the software requirements for ThinkMedia: An Integrated Platform for OJT Preparation, Career Development, and Opportunities, version 1.0. It is a web-based integrated platform for OJT preparation, career development, and discovery of opportunities. College students preparing for On-the-Job Training (OJT) may have difficulty finding opportunities and accessing useful information about workplace skills, expectations, and application processes. At the same time, students are increasingly using social media as a source of information and learning. ThinkMedia aims to use a social-media-style platform to provide students with useful career and OJT-related content, such as workplace tips, skill guides, career insights, and application advice. The platform will also provide an AI assistant for guidance and allow students to discover and apply for OJT opportunities posted by participating companies and organizations. This SRS covers all the features of ThinkMedia, including user registration and profile, company dashboard and portal, career OJT feed, OJT preparation resources, and AI career assistant. Moreover, this also applies to three user groups: students, instructors, and participating companies or organizations.

P50 normal 1.4	Product Scope
P51 normal ThinkMedia is an interactive platform that intends to help college students prepare for OJT and future careers through the combination of career-related content, AI-assisted guidance, and direct access to OJT opportunities and networking in a single application/system tailored similarly to social media but attuned locally. The platform was realized primarily through the observations that students preparing for OJT struggle with finding opportunities, which is already combined with how stressful OJT is for students, festering from several factors like lack of pre-deployment information, inadequate supervision and even task misalignments (Hingpit, 2024)[4} which lead to severe burnout (Ochoa et al., 2025)l[1] Career counselling as well, through AI chatbots or hybrid models (human/AI) frameworks are becoming increasingly relevant as proponents  (Suresh et al., 2021)[2] to solve these problems focused on finding relevant OJT workplaces and even towards future careers especially when focused towards university students (Sarmurzin et al., 2026)[3]. Similarly, students turn to social media as primary channels for gathering career-related information or even positions, and which actually lead to positively influencing the employment choices available for students and their confidence within this (Li et al., 2025)[5].
P53 normal Given this, the product's primary scope includes the following systems:
P55 normal User Registration/Profiles – Allows users to sign up, enter basic information like age and purpose of using the platform and use these to personalize the content, recommendations and OJT opportunities as they are shown.
P57 normal Learning Mode – Personalized educational feeds that shows trivia, short lessons and quizzes/challenges to build general knowledge and career-relevant skills.
P59 normal OJT Portal – Dedicated space for OJT-adjacent preparation to build up workplace readiness and exploration for listings, company information and apply directly to opportunities through the platform.
P61 normal AI Assistant/Chatbot – Answers questions and relevant queries, provides guidance and support to users.
P63 normal Company Portal – Used by participating companies and organizations to post OJT opportunities, which may also be aggregated from social media platforms, view applicant profiles, submissions and manage and review these applications.

P970 Heading 2 7. Software Architecture and Detailed Design Package
P971 Heading 3 7.1 Architecture
P972 normal ThinkMedia has three tiers: React for the interface, FastAPI for application logic, and PostgreSQL for data. The backend keeps accounts, learning, applications, recommendations, AI, and administration in separate modules within one application.
P973 normal React communicates with FastAPI over HTTPS. The server accesses PostgreSQL, protects uploaded files, and calls the LLM with approved content. API keys stay on the server.
P974 Heading 3 7.2 Component Responsibilities
P975 normal Modules follow the PD5 requirements: accounts (FR01–02), learning (FR03), opportunities/applications (FR04–07), recommendations (FR08–09), assistant (FR10), and administration/evaluation (FR11–12). They share validation, logging, and file-access controls.
P976 normal Ranking combines normalized scores: score = alpha × contentScore + (1 − alpha) × collaborativeScore. Choose alpha using validation data; use alpha = 1 when interactions are insufficient. Exclude unavailable items and show popular eligible content when profile data is missing.
P977 normal The assistant uses approved resources to answer questions with source references. It treats retrieved text as reference material and cannot change database records.
P978 Heading 3 7.3 Deployment Design
P979 normal The browser loads React from a web host. FastAPI runs on a Linux host and connects privately to PostgreSQL. Resumes stay in private storage with authorized downloads. The external LLM is accessed through its API.
P980 normal Keep development and pilot settings separate. Before deployment, apply migrations, test core workflows, and prepare rollback. Back up the database daily and test restoration.

P984 Heading 3 7.5 Data and API Design

ADR03 | Start with profile-based recommendations and add collaborative signals as data grows. A collaborative-only model would struggle with new users.
ADR04 | Use an external LLM with approved content instead of training a model. This reduces development effort but requires cost limits, timeouts, and failure handling.
ADR05 | Store attachments privately, not as public uploads. Authorize downloads and remove files according to retention rules.
ADR06 | Choose hosting and LLM providers before deployment. Record their cost limits, region, retention terms, quotas, and tested response times.
